Apollo missions · Moon

ALSEP: A science station left behind

Deployed 1969–1972Shut down 1977

The Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package was a group of instruments astronauts installed so scientists could keep studying the Moon after the crews returned to Earth.

Discover its story

ALSEP instruments investigated lunar quakes, heat flow, magnetic fields and the environment around the landing sites. The stations were designed for about one year of operation (two years for Apollo 17), but several operated for years. NASA shut down the five ALSEP stations on September 30, 1977.

Why it matters: The instruments turned the Moon into a long-running scientific observation site, not just a place astronauts briefly visited.

Apollo 11 · Moon

The lunar retroreflector

Placed 1969Science continues

A compact array of special mirrors was placed on the Moon to reflect laser pulses sent from Earth.

Discover its story

By measuring how long a laser pulse takes to travel to the reflector and return, scientists can measure the Earth–Moon distance with great precision. Unlike many powered experiments, the passive reflector needs no electrical power. Lunar laser-ranging research using Apollo-era reflectors continues today.

Why it matters: It helps scientists study the Moon's orbit, rotation and the Earth–Moon system.

Mars Pathfinder · 1997

Sojourner: The little trailblazer

Landed July 4, 199783 days of rover operations

Sojourner was the first successful wheeled rover to explore the surface of Mars. It studied nearby rocks and soil and sent images and measurements to Earth through its lander.

Discover its story

The rover was planned for a seven-day primary mission but operated for 83 days. The final Pathfinder data transmission arrived on September 27, 1997. The mission was formally terminated on March 10, 1998, after communication could not be restored.

Why it matters: Sojourner demonstrated that a small mobile robot could explore another planet and examine different surface targets.

Mars Exploration Rover · Spirit

Spirit: The long-lived geologist

Landed January 2004Mission ended May 2011

Spirit explored rocks and soil to investigate whether Mars once had environments where liquid water could exist.

Discover its story

Spirit operated for more than six years, far beyond its planned 90-day mission. It found strong evidence that ancient Mars was much wetter than it is today. The rover became stuck in soft soil and stopped communicating in March 2010. NASA ended recovery efforts on May 25, 2011.

Why it matters: Spirit's discoveries helped scientists understand the planet's watery past and changing environment.

Mars Exploration Rover · Opportunity

Opportunity: A record-breaking explorer

Landed January 2004Mission ended February 2019

Opportunity travelled across Mars, studying rocks and searching for clues about ancient water.

Discover its story

Opportunity operated for almost 15 years and travelled more than 28 miles (45 kilometres). It found evidence that ancient Martian environments may once have been able to support microbial life. A planet-wide dust storm in 2018 blocked sunlight from its solar panels. NASA declared the mission over on February 13, 2019.

Why it matters: Its long journey transformed scientists' understanding of water-related environments on Mars.

A journey through time

Mission timeline

From the first Apollo science packages to the final message from Opportunity, each mission added a new chapter to planetary exploration.

1969

Apollo 11 places a laser retroreflector on the Moon

The passive reflector makes precise Earth–Moon laser ranging possible.

1969–1972

Apollo crews deploy ALSEP stations

ALSEP stations are installed at the Apollo 12, 14, 15, 16 and 17 landing sites.

September 30, 1977

ALSEP stations are shut down

NASA ends operations of the five ALSEP stations after years of lunar science.

July 4, 1997

Mars Pathfinder lands with Sojourner

The mission demonstrates a new way to land and operate a small rover on Mars.

January 2004

Spirit and Opportunity reach Mars

The twin rovers begin exploring different regions of the Red Planet.

May 25, 2011

Spirit's mission is declared complete

After more than six years, NASA ends efforts to contact the rover.

February 13, 2019

Opportunity's mission is declared complete

After almost 15 years, NASA concludes the mission following a prolonged loss of communication.

More than machines

What did they teach us?

These missions show that equipment can continue to create scientific value long after its original plan has ended.

Listen to a world

Lunar seismic instruments helped scientists investigate vibrations and the Moon's internal structure.

Measure across space

Laser retroreflectors allow researchers to measure the Earth–Moon distance with high precision.

Read the rocks

Mars rovers examined rocks and soil for clues about the planet's ancient environments and water history.

Why remember abandoned equipment?

Because a mission's end is not the end of its story. The data, discoveries, engineering lessons and physical artifacts continue to help future generations understand how we explore space.
